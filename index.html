💡 How the Forecast Works

The AI model calculates a shortage risk score (0–100%) for each of the 8 blood types by evaluating three key factors:

●Current Stock Level — Lower stock = exponentially higher risk. Types with 0–2 units are flagged as critical.

●Blood Type Rarity — Negative blood types (O-, B-, AB-) are inherently rarer in the donor pool and receive an elevated baseline risk premium.

●Requisition Velocity — A high volume of recent hospital requests for a specific type accelerates its projected depletion timeline.

🤖 If a Gemini API key is configured in .env, the model uses generative AI for dynamic, context-aware forecasting. Otherwise, a high-fidelity local heuristics engine is used.

🔬 Key Clinical Facts
🩸 O− Universal Donor
O negative blood can be transfused to any patient in an emergency regardless of their blood type, making it critically valuable in trauma situations.
💜 AB+ Universal Recipient
AB positive patients can safely receive any blood type. AB+ plasma can also be donated universally.
⚠️ Negative Types Are Rarer
Negative blood types (O−, A−, B−, AB−) represent ~15% of the population, making them harder to source and higher risk for shortages.
🗓️ 90-Day Donation Interval
Whole blood donors must wait a minimum of 90 days (about 3 months) between donations to allow full haemoglobin recovery.
